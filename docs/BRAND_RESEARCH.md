## Logo and identity (from the 150×150 profile image)

- Circular badge on a cream/ivory field with a thin gold ring.
- A woman's profile in line art, hair turning into olive-green leaves, with small gold botanical accents.
- "ELIXIRA" set in a classical high-contrast serif, olive/dark green, with "AYURVEDA" in small spaced capitals below.
- Faint small text runs along the lower ring. **It is illegible at this size**, so its wording is unknown.
- Observed colours: cream, olive/forest green, muted gold. This lines up well with the proposed palette, **except the logo uses gold, which the proposed palette lacks.**

The product labels also carry a "Dr. Tanzeel's" mark above the logo (visible on the Black Seed Hair Oil bottle).

## Brand messaging themes

1. Physician-made: the doctor's name and degree appear on every product post. This is the strongest trust signal the brand has.
2. Homemade / small batch: "A new batch … is ready". This matches the ERP batch model well.
3. Herb-rich formulas: "20 herbs", "15–20 essential herbs", named herbs listed.
4. Hair care first. "Clear skin" is in the bio, but **no skin product appeared in the 12 visible posts.**

## Assumptions (not verified)

- Whether the business holds an AYUSH manufacturing licence. See Risks.

## Compliance risks in the current messaging

These are not legal advice. A qualified reviewer should confirm before launch.

| Claim seen | Concern | Website recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| "Enhances hair growth", "Prevents premature greying", "Reduces hair fall" | Ayurvedic products sold in India fall under the Drugs & Cosmetics Act / AYUSH rules. Disease and cure claims are restricted, and the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act applies. | Use cosmetic, experience-based wording ("nourishes the scalp", "for fuller-looking hair") until claims are reviewed. |
| "antimicrobial", "Anti-dandruff" | Therapeutic claim | Same |
| "Chemical free" | Scientifically meaningless, and ASCI flags it as misleading. | Use "No synthetic fragrance" or "No added preservatives" **only if true** for that SKU. |
| "Heal Deeply" (tagline) | Can be read as a therapeutic promise | Keep it, but never pair it with disease or condition words on product pages. Or consider "Glow Naturally. Nourish Deeply." |
| Swarna Prashana for children | A medical service for minors; it should not be sold as an e-commerce product. | Show it only as a clinic service with an enquiry/WhatsApp CTA. Exclude it from v1 checkout. |
| "Homemade" | Conflicts with "premium international brand" and with licensed manufacturing | Reframe as "small-batch, hand-prepared under physician supervision" if accurate. |
